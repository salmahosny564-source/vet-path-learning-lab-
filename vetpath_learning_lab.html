Core diagnostic principle
Hyperplasia is not synonymous with inflammation. Identify inflammatory cells and tissue injury before assigning an inflammatory diagnosis.

Selected lesions
Important distinction: In bitches, cystic endometrial hyperplasia (CEH) is a proliferative change associated with hormonal influences; pyometra is a separate clinical syndrome involving uterine infection and inflammation. They can coexist but are not interchangeable diagnoses.

Comparative pathology

Cow vs. bitch

Typical patterns and diagnostic implications; individual cases may vary.

FeatureCow (bovine)Bitch (canine)
Common contextPostpartum uterine recovery, retained fetal membranes, dystocia, and ascending infection may predispose to inflammation.Diestrus and progesterone-associated uterine changes are relevant to CEH–pyometra complex; age and reproductive history matter.
Inflammatory lesionsEndometritis; metritis, especially postpartum; chronic endometrial inflammation.Endometritis; pyometra-associated inflammation; severity varies with disease process.
Non-inflammatory changesEndometrial fibrosis, cystic gland dilation, glandular atrophy, and other structural changes.Cystic endometrial hyperplasia (CEH), glandular atrophy, and other non-inflammatory changes.
Gross cluesEnlargement and abnormal exudate may occur with metritis; chronic lesions may be subtle.CEH may show cystic mucosal changes; pyometra may cause uterine distension and purulent contents.
Histology: inflammationInflammatory infiltrates, epithelial injury, and distribution/depth help characterize the lesion.Inflammatory infiltrates with possible glandular or epithelial injury; correlate clinically.
Histology: non-inflammatoryFibrosis, dilated glands, reduced glandular activity, or proliferation without a primary inflammatory infiltrate.Dilated endometrial glands and variable proliferation in CEH; assess separately for inflammation.
Useful investigationsReproductive history, examination, uterine sampling when appropriate, cytology, histopathology, and culture/PCR where indicated.Clinical examination, imaging, laboratory findings, and histopathology when available and appropriate.
Diagnostic cautionPostpartum disease categories have specific clinical definitions; do not diagnose from gross appearance alone.CEH alone does not establish pyometra. Pyometra is a clinical disease, not merely a histological synonym for CEH.
Inflammatory pattern

Cells + injury + distribution

Describe cell type, location and extent, epithelial integrity, stromal changes, and whether the myometrium is involved. Neutrophils often support active suppurative inflammation; chronic lesions may contain lymphocytes, plasma cells, and macrophages.

Non-inflammatory pattern

Architecture + glands + stroma

Assess gland number and shape, luminal dilation, epithelial height, stromal fibrosis, and atrophy. Proliferative or cystic changes may coexist with inflammation, so describe each component separately.
